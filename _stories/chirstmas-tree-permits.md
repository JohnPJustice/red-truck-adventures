Bringing home a Christmas tree from the national forest is a Pacific Northwest tradition. The Gifford Pinchot National Forest has its 2026 permits on sale now.

The Forest Service announced on November 10 that in-person sales begin **Thursday, November 12**. Permits are sold at three ranger district offices and more than a dozen local stores. You can also buy one online now through Recreation.gov.

## The basics

- **Price:** $5 per permit. Offices accept cash, check, or credit card. Online purchases may carry extra administrative fees.
- **Limit:** up to **five permits per household**, and each permit covers one tree.
- **What comes with it:** a map of areas closed to cutting, plus instructions and harvesting guidelines.
- **Carry it:** you must have a **printed copy** of the permit with you while you cut.
- **Fourth graders cut free:** through the Every Kid Outdoors program, a fourth grader with a paper voucher from [everykidoutdoors.gov](https://everykidoutdoors.gov/index.htm) can get a free permit at any national forest office.

The Forest Service asks buyers to read the permit overview and important information online before purchasing.

## Where to buy one in person

**Forest Service offices** are open Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and close from noon to 1 p.m. for lunch. All offices are **closed Thanksgiving Day, November 26**.

- Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument & District Office, Amboy
- Cowlitz Valley Ranger District, Randle (on US 12)
- Mt. Adams Ranger District, Trout Lake (on Highway 141)

**Local vendors** in the release include stores in Amboy, Ashford, Carson, Cougar, Elbe, Kalama, Morton, Packwood, Randle, Stevenson, Trout Lake, and Vancouver. Hours vary, so the Forest Service suggests calling ahead. The full list with phone numbers is in the [official release](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/giffordpinchot/newsroom/releases/gifford-pinchot-national-forest-2026-christmas-tree-permits).

## The drive is part of the trip

This is where the truck comes in. The Forest Service warns that **many forest roads are not maintained for winter travel**, and the weather in the mountains can change fast. Their recommended kit:

- Traction devices and a shovel
- Extra food and water
- Warm clothing and blankets
- A flashlight and a first-aid kit
- A saw or other cutting tool
- Rope or straps to secure the tree for the drive home

The agency also notes that cutting a tree usually takes longer than people expect. Start early and tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back. Plan to be **out of the forest well before dark**.

Before you head up, check the forest's [road repairs and closures page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/giffordpinchot/alerts/road-repairs-road-closures-2026). For state highways on the way in, check the [WSDOT travel map](https://wsdot.com/travel/real-time/map/).

## A few of our own reminders

- Stick to the areas your permit map shows as open. Stay off private land and anywhere marked closed.
- Pull fully off the road before you stop, and only where it's legal and safe to park.
- Strap the tree down well. A tree that shifts on a wet highway is a hazard to everyone behind you.
