The DJI Mini 4 Pro replaced the Mini 3 Pro in late 2023 and added omnidirectional obstacle sensing — the one thing the Mini 3 Pro clearly needed. After using it for several months across a range of Pacific Northwest environments, the short version is: it's the right drone for this kind of work.

## Image Quality

The 4K/60fps mode with 10-bit D-Log M gives you real latitude to pull the PNW greens and grays into something that doesn't look oversaturated or flat. The dynamic range is noticeably better than the Mini 3 Pro in high-contrast situations — tree canopy against a bright sky, for example, or a river surface lit from one side.

The stabilization is solid in calm conditions. In wind above about 15 mph, you start to see microjiggles in longer focal-length shots. Not a problem at wide angle, which is where most of the useful shots are anyway.

## Obstacle Sensing in Forests

The omnidirectional sensing is the reason to choose this over the Mini 3 Pro for wooded environments. Flying in and around tree canopy on the Cowlitz River required the sensing to catch a branch on one pass — it stopped the drone about 18 inches out. Without sensing, that's a lost drone.

It won't replace careful flying. The sensors don't see thin branches well, and in heavy rain they're unreliable. But for the kind of cautious low-altitude work this site does, they're a meaningful safety net.

## Practical Notes

The battery performs to spec in warm weather. In 45°F conditions — which is most of the year out here — plan on 25 minutes of actual flight time. Carry two batteries minimum.

The Intelligent Flight Modes (Dronie, Spotlight, etc.) are more useful than they look. The Hyperlapse mode is solid for river and sky shots.

The RC2 controller with a built-in screen is worth the $100 upgrade. In bright Pacific Northwest summer days, the phone screen on the RC-N1 is nearly unreadable.

## Bottom Line

The DJI Mini 4 Pro is the right drone for documented adventure work in the Pacific Northwest. The weight keeps it manageable for day hikes. The image quality is professional enough to stand alongside ground-level footage without obvious quality differences. The sensing makes wooded flying survivable.
