The stretch of road along the Cowlitz River south of Kelso doesn't look like a destination. It's utilitarian — built for access to industrial properties and river infrastructure, not for scenic drives. But it runs right along the water for several miles, and the combination of the industrial shoreline and the wide, slow river is exactly the kind of thing that doesn't show up on travel sites.

## The Drive

Start at the Kelso boat launch on South Kelso Road and head south along the river. The road stays close to the water for most of the first two miles. There are several places to pull off and get down to the bank — most are gravel turnouts that have been used long enough to be worn flat.

The river here runs gray-green in winter and early spring. The Cowlitz carries a lot of silt after rain events, which changes the color. In late summer it drops and clears. Both versions are worth filming.

## Industrial Context

The Cowlitz south of Kelso has been a working river for a long time. You'll pass an active aggregate facility, storage yards, and a few structures that look like they haven't changed in fifty years. None of this is hidden — it's right on the road. But it gives the drive a texture that purely scenic roads don't have.

There's a log storage area mid-drive that still sees occasional use. Log rafts on Pacific Northwest rivers are becoming rare; this is one of the few spots where you might still see one.

## River Access

The best unimproved access is about 1.8 miles south of the boat launch, where a gravel road leads down to a cobble beach. The beach is public — below the ordinary high water mark on navigable rivers is open to the public in Washington. It's a good spot to stop and get the wide-angle shot of the river.

## Notes on Filming

This drive films best in overcast conditions. Clear days flatten the river color. Overcast gives you the gray-green water, the industrial gray of the shoreline structures, and a sky that doesn't compete.

The drone shot from the high bank on the south end shows the river bend and the I-5 bridge in the same frame. That's the establishing shot worth getting.

## Getting There

From downtown Kelso: South on South Kelso Road to the boat launch. The drive continues south from there. There's no loop — you turn around and come back, which gives you the other bank of light on the return.
